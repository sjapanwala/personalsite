Day 1 Arrival Friday, Jul 25
Afternoon
Land + transfer to hotel Transit
Narita: Narita Express to Shinjuku (~55 min, ¥3,070). Haneda: Keikyu Line to Shinagawa (~15 min, ¥330). Check in, freshen up. Recommended neighbourhood: Shinjuku or Shibuya for central access all week.
6:30 pm
Omoide Yokocho — Memory Lane
Tiny yakitori stalls spilling smoke into narrow alleyways just outside Shinjuku Station west exit. Order skewers and cold Sapporo. Dirt cheap and atmospheric — the perfect first night in Tokyo.
8:30 pm
Early night — beat jet lag
Big days ahead. Target 10pm local time to reset your clock. The next 11 days will be exhausting in the best way.
Day 2 Shibuya + Harajuku Saturday, Jul 26
8:00 am
Meiji Shrine
Best in the morning before heat. Forested walk leads to a massive torii gate and a serene Shinto shrine. Free entry. Takes ~45 min including the approach path.
10:00 am
Harajuku — Takeshita Street
Quirky street fashion, crepe shops, candy stores. Wild people watching. Gets very packed by noon so arriving at 10 is ideal. Ura-Harajuku back streets are more low-key and interesting for actual fashion.
12:00 pm
Omotesando lunch
Tokyo's most architecturally interesting boulevard. Dozens of well-designed cafés. Try Afuri ramen (light yuzu broth, popular with locals) or any of the smaller lunch spots off the main drag.
2:00 pm
Shibuya Crossing Must
Watch from street level first — stand on the island and let the crowd swirl around you. Then head to Scramble Square Sky Lobby (47F, free observation area). Best crossing photos from the 2nd floor of Mag's Park in Shibuya Hikarie building.
4:00 pm
Shibuya 109 + side streets
Browse the iconic fashion tower and the smaller boutiques behind it — record shops, vintage stores, sneaker spots. The streets between Shibuya and Daikanyama are excellent for walking.
7:00 pm
Dinner + rooftop drinks
Ichiran ramen (famous solo booths — each person eats in a private cubicle, order by form) or Gyukatsu Motomura for crispy beef cutlet you cook yourself on a hot stone. After: rooftop bar at Cerulean Tower for city views.
Day 3 Akihabara + Shinjuku Night Sunday, Jul 27
10:00 am
Akihabara Electric Town Must
The world's largest consumer electronics district. Start at Yodobashi Camera Akiba (9 floors — cameras, drones, components, everything). Then hit: Sofmap for second-hand electronics and cameras, Super Potato for retro games and consoles (Famicom, Super Famicom, old handhelds), Akky International for duty-free deals on cameras and lenses. The back alleys have small specialist shops for cables, ICs, and niche components — genuinely unlike anything in Canada.
1:00 pm
Lunch in Akihabara
Try a maid café for exactly 30 min of cultural experience (Maidreamin is the most mainstream entry point). Or just grab karaage lunch at one of the basement restaurants around the station.
3:00 pm
Ueno optional detour
15 min walk from Akihabara. Tokyo National Museum (vast collection, worth 1.5h), Ueno Park, Ameya-Yokocho open market street. Good cultural break if you're all teched out.
6:00 pm
Shinjuku Kabukicho + Golden Gai
Tokyo's entertainment district at night. Walk through the neon-drenched streets past Kabukicho Tower. Then find Golden Gai — a labyrinth of 200+ bars, each seating 4–8 people. Every bar has its own theme and personality. Pick one with a door that speaks to you and have two drinks there. The most memorable night-out experience in Japan.
Day 4 Tokyo Disneyland Tuesday, Jul 28
7:00 am
Depart for Maihama Transit
JR Keiyo Line from Tokyo Station to Maihama (~25 min). Walk to the gates. Queue forms 30–45 min before opening. Park opens 8am in summer — weekday but July is still busy.
8:00 am
Tokyo Disneyland — full day Must Book tickets in advance
Priority rides on entry: Big Thunder Mountain, Haunted Mansion, Space Mountain, Splash Mountain. Use Disney Premier Access (paid, ¥1,500–2,000/ride) for top attractions — worth it in summer. Must eat: Monte Cristo Sandwich at Café Orléans, churros, Queen of Hearts Banquet Hall for a sit-down meal. Stay for the evening electrical parade (10pm) and fireworks. Last train back around 11:30pm.
Day 5 Asakusa + Skytree + teamLab Wednesday, Jul 29
8:00 am
Senso-ji Temple, Asakusa
Tokyo's oldest and most visited temple. Go early before the crowds. Thunder Gate (Kaminarimon) for the classic photo, then walk Nakamise-dori shopping street for souvenir shopping — good prices here. Rickshaw rides available if you want the experience (~¥3,000 for 30 min).
10:30 am
Tokyo Skytree Book timed entry
634m — world's tallest tower. 5 min walk from Senso-ji. Two observation decks: Tembo Deck (350m, ¥2,100) and Tembo Galleria (450m, +¥1,000). Book timed entry online to avoid queues. Glass floor panels at 350m are worth it. Clear day = Mt. Fuji visible on the horizon.
12:30 pm
Lunch — Sumida River area
Good soba and tempura spots along the riverside between Asakusa and Skytree. Sit down and rest — Disneyland was yesterday and you have teamLab this afternoon.
3:00 pm
teamLab Planets, Toyosu Must Book 3+ weeks ahead
Walk-through immersive digital art museum. You go barefoot — rooms with water floors, infinite mirror ceilings, fields of floating flowers. Different from any museum you've been to. ~20 min from Asakusa by metro. Sells out weeks in advance in summer. Pre-book at teamlab.art. Evening slot recommended for the light experience.
7:00 pm
Tsukiji Outer Market dinner
Tsukiji is near Toyosu by taxi (~15 min). The outer market has excellent standing sushi bars and grilled seafood stalls open into the evening. Great final Tokyo dinner.
Day 6 Morning Tokyo → Shinkansen to Kyoto Thursday, Jul 30
7:30 am
Tsukiji Outer Market breakfast
Japan's most famous seafood market outer section opens at 5am. Fresh uni, tuna sashimi, oysters, tamagoyaki (sweet rolled egg on a stick). The best breakfast of the trip. Get there early — stalls can sell out by 9am.
10:30 am
Shinkansen Nozomi — Tokyo → Kyoto ~2h 15min
From Tokyo Station. Book reserved seats (included with JR Pass — do this at the station on arrival day). Sit on row D or E, right side for a Mt. Fuji view ~45 min after departure on a clear day. Buy an ekiben (station bento box) from the station shops before boarding — eating on the shinkansen is part of the experience.
1:00 pm
Arrive Kyoto — check in
Stay near Gion or Kawaramachi for the best walking access. A traditional machiya guesthouse (old townhouse) is worth the upgrade here — sets the tone for Kyoto immediately.
2:30 pm
Nishiki Market
Kyoto's Kitchen — a covered 5-block market dating to the 17th century. Try: yuzu pickles, matcha mochi, fresh tofu skewers, dashi-marinated eggs. Closes around 6pm so go now, not later. Great for afternoon snacking.
5:00 pm
Gion evening walk
Hanamikoji Street — walk slowly between 5:30–6:30pm. Best chance to spot geiko (geisha) and maiko (apprentices) heading to evening engagements. No flash photography. Shimbashi area for the lantern-lit canal path — one of the most photographed streets in Japan.
7:30 pm
Pontocho Alley dinner
One of Japan's most atmospheric dining streets — narrow lantern-lit alley parallel to the Kamo River. Restaurants have kawayuka — riverside terraces over the water. Try kaiseki cuisine for a proper introduction to Kyoto cooking. Budget ¥3,000–6,000/person for a set menu.
Shinkansen Nozomi — Tokyo → Kyoto · ~2h 15min · JR Pass · Reserve seats at station on arrival day
City 02

Kyoto

3 nights
Jul 30 (Thu) – Aug 2 (Sat)
Day 7 Fushimi Inari + Arashiyama Friday, Jul 31
6:30 am
Fushimi Inari Taisha Go at dawn — non-negotiable
Arrive 6:30–7am. Thousands of vermilion torii gates winding up the mountain — completely crowd-free at this hour, ethereal mist in the trees. Full summit hike: 2–3 hours (233m), brings you back by ~9:30am before the heat becomes brutal. Minimum: hike 30–45 min to the upper viewing area at Yotsutsuji (4-way intersection) for the best city views. The gate tunnels are fully shaded and the early morning light through the orange wood is genuinely one of the best sights in Japan.
10:00 am
Rest + hotel breakfast
You'll be hot and you've been hiking. Take an hour. Arashiyama in the afternoon is better when the bamboo light is softer anyway.
1:00 pm
Arashiyama Bamboo Grove Must
JR Sagano Line (~25 min, JR Pass). Walk the bamboo path — short (10 min) but remarkable. Immediately after: Tenryu-ji Zen garden (¥500, one of Japan's finest moss gardens). Then walk down to the Oi River — rent a rowboat for ¥1,200/hour or just walk the riverside.
3:30 pm
Saga Toriimoto preserved street
10 min walk from the bamboo grove. A preserved 19th century merchant street — traditional sake shops, craft stores, matcha tea houses. Far fewer tourists than the main Arashiyama strip and better for photos.
6:00 pm
Return + Gion izakaya dinner
Head back to central Kyoto. Izakaya dinner in Gion — grilled skewers, cold Sapporo, small plates. Relaxed evening after a big day.
Day 8 Golden Pavilion + Philosopher's Path + Kiyomizudera Saturday, Aug 1
9:00 am
Kinkaku-ji — Golden Pavilion Must
Opens 9am — be there at opening. Gold leaf temple reflecting perfectly in the Mirror Pond. Gets very crowded by 10:30am so this timing matters. The surrounding garden walk takes ~25 min. ¥500 entrance.
10:30 am
Ryoan-ji Rock Garden
15 min from Kinkaku-ji. The most famous dry rock garden in Japan — 15 stones arranged so you can never see all 15 from a single viewpoint. Oddly compelling to stare at for 20 min. The large moss garden behind the main hall is underrated and usually quieter. ¥600.
12:00 pm
Philosopher's Path + lunch
A 2km stone path along a cherry-tree-lined canal. Start at Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion, ¥500) and walk south. Dozens of small cafés and artisan workshops along the way — take your time and duck into anything that looks interesting. Best shaded afternoon walk in Kyoto.
2:30 pm
Nanzen-ji Temple
End of the Philosopher's Path. Massive sanmon gate you can climb for views over the trees. The highlight: a 19th century red brick aqueduct running directly through the temple grounds — completely incongruous and weirdly beautiful. Sub-temple Tenjuan has one of Kyoto's best gardens. ¥600.
5:00 pm
Kiyomizudera at golden hour Must
Iconic wooden stage temple on a forested hillside — open late in summer. Walk up the stone-paved Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka lanes lined with traditional shops (best souvenir shopping in Kyoto). The main stage has sweeping city views best in golden hour. ¥400 entrance. Get matcha soft serve from a vendor on the walk up.
8:00 pm
Kawaramachi night — Japanese whisky bars
Kyoto's nightlife district. Try Bar K6 or Zoetrope for an extraordinary Japanese whisky selection. Highball culture is huge here — order a Yamazaki or Nikka Coffey Grain highball. Great way to end Kyoto.
Day 9 Morning Kyoto → Osaka Sunday, Aug 2
8:45 am
Nijo Castle
Former Tokugawa shogun residence — famous for its "nightingale floors" (boards designed to squeak to alert against intruders). Ornate painted sliding doors inside, large stone garden. Opens 8:45am. ~1.5h. ¥800.
11:00 am
Check out + JR to Osaka ~30 min
JR Kyoto–Osaka line (JR Pass) from Kyoto Station to Osaka Station. Check into hotel — stay near Namba or Shinsaibashi for Dotonbori walking distance. Drop bags and head straight out.
2:00 pm
Dotonbori first walk Must
The canal strip — Glico running man sign, giant crab and fugu signs, neon everywhere. Best street food strip in Japan: takoyaki (octopus balls — get them from Aizuya, the original), okonomiyaki (savory pancake, cook your own), kushikatsu (battered skewers — no double dipping the sauce, it's a rule). Walk both sides of the canal completely.
5:00 pm
Shinsaibashi shopping arcade
600m covered shopping arcade — good for gifts and last-minute Japanese fashion brands. Connects to Amerikamura (American Village) for streetwear and vintage. Great air-conditioned break from the heat.
8:00 pm
Osaka-style dinner — eat aggressively
Osaka is Japan's food capital — locals call it "kuidaore" (eat until you drop). Try a proper sit-down okonomiyaki restaurant where you cook on a table grill, or find a standing teppanyaki spot in Dotonbori. Spend more on food here than anywhere else on the trip.
JR Kyoto Line — Kyoto → Osaka · ~30 min · JR Pass · Or Hankyu Railway (~¥400, no pass needed, slightly faster)

Osaka

3 nights + departure
Aug 2 (Sat) – Aug 6 (Wed)
Day 10 Osaka Castle + Kuromon + Den Den Town Monday, Aug 3
9:00 am
Osaka Castle
Go early before the heat. Striking castle (rebuilt 1931) in large park grounds — the moat corners give the best exterior photos. Museum inside covers the castle's history through 7 floors with good English signage. ¥600. ~1.5h total.
11:30 am
Kuromon Ichiba Market — lunch Must
Osaka's kitchen — 180+ stalls of fresh seafood, wagyu beef skewers, fresh crab legs, oysters. Eat as you walk. Vendors hand you samples. Try: fresh uni (sea urchin) on a cracker, wagyu skewer on the grill (~¥1,500), whole crab grilled while you wait. The most concentrated food experience in Osaka outside Dotonbori.
1:30 pm
Den Den Town Must
Osaka's electronics and anime district on Nipponbashi Street. Less touristy than Akihabara — locals actually shop here. Joshin Super Kids Land: excellent for retro games and figures. Nankai department store electronics floor. Good for final tech shopping — often slightly cheaper than Akihabara for certain items. Check voltage compatibility before buying electronics.
4:00 pm
Shinsekai retro district
Old-school Osaka neighbourhood under Tsutenkaku Tower. Very local atmosphere — old men playing shogi, cheap kushikatsu restaurants, vintage arcades. Completely different vibe from touristy Dotonbori. Fugu (pufferfish) restaurants are everywhere here — try it if you're adventurous.
7:00 pm
Namba evening — canal by night
Return to Dotonbori for dinner. The canal at night with all signs lit is the best version of it. Kani Doraku crab restaurant, or standing yakiniku for wagyu grilled at the counter.
Day 11 Universal Studios Japan + Super Nintendo World Tuesday, Aug 4
7:00 am
Depart early — JR Osaka Loop Line ~15 min
JR Osaka Loop Line to Universal City Station (JR Pass). Aim to arrive at gates 15 min before park opening (8am in summer). Weekday helps but July is peak — early arrival is critical.
8:00 am
Super Nintendo World — FIRST Go here immediately Book tickets in advance
Run to Nintendo World the moment gates open. It requires an Area Timed Entry pass — grab it on the USJ app or kiosks immediately on entry. These sell out within 20 minutes. Do not go to any other ride first. Mario Kart: Koopa's Challenge is the main AR ride (use Express Pass or expect 90+ min queue). Donkey Kong Country expansion (2024) — Mine Cart Madness coaster is worth the wait. Buy the Power-Up Band wristband (¥3,500) — tracks coin blocks, boss battles, syncs with Nintendo Account. Eat at Kinopio's Cafe for Question Block Tiramisu and Mushroom Soup.
8–11:30 am
Nintendo World deep dive — spend 3 hours minimum
Mario Kart ride, Yoshi's Adventure coaster, Donkey Kong coaster, all the interactive coin blocks throughout the land. Compete in boss battles against Bowser. Get photos everywhere — this is a once-in-a-trip experience. Check coin total on your wristband app.
11:30 am
Wizarding World of Harry Potter Must
Hogsmeade Village is stunning architecture. Get: Frozen Butterbeer (better than cold), watch the Ollivanders wand shop show. Ride: Harry Potter and the Forbidden Journey (walk through Hogwarts + simulator — incredible) and Flight of the Hippogriff outdoor coaster. Use Express Pass for Harry Potter ride — midday queues hit 2 hours.
1:30 pm
Lunch + midday rest
Three Broomsticks (Harry Potter zone) for a full sit-down meal. Sit down for 45 min during the hottest part of the afternoon (2–3pm) — July at USJ is brutal heat.
3:00 pm
Hollywood Dream + Jurassic Park
Hollywood Dream coaster (backwards option is better — ask for it), Jurassic Park The Ride if you want to get soaked (worth it in the heat). Minion Park for Despicable Me ride.
5:00 pm
Return to Nintendo World — evening pass
Queues drop significantly after 4:30pm. Get back on Mario Kart, hit any coin blocks you missed, explore Donkey Kong area again. The land looks different with evening lighting — stay until close for the full experience.
7:30 pm
Evening parade + return
USJ summer evening show — check schedule at entrance. Last trains back from Universal City ~11:30pm.
Day 12 Umeda + Final Shopping + Farewell Dinner Wednesday, Aug 5
10:00 am
Umeda Sky Building — floating garden
Two towers connected at the top by an open circular sky walkway — 360° Osaka views, more intimate than Skytree. Looks especially good in late morning before haze builds. ¥1,500. Underground "Takimi-koji" alley below the building has excellent ramen shops for lunch after.
12:00 pm
Osaka Station + Daimaru final shopping
Last chance for quality Japanese purchases. Uniqlo flagship, Muji, Japanese skincare and cosmetics, Kit-Kat Japan exclusive flavours (matcha, sake, wasabi — great gifts). Osaka omiyage to bring home: Baton d'Or cookies, Glico caramel, takoyaki kits.
3:00 pm
Dotonbori final afternoon walk
Last stroll. Pick up anything missed. Get matcha soft serve or taiyaki (fish-shaped cake, red bean or custard) for the walk. Good photo light before the evening crowds.
7:00 pm
Farewell dinner — splurge Book 1 week ahead
Osaka is Japan's food capital. Do not eat cheap tonight. Options: Yoroniku (exceptional wagyu yakiniku, ¥15,000+/person), Fujiya 1935 (Michelin 2-star kaiseki, reservations essential), or a high-end sushi omakase counter. Budget ¥10,000–20,000/person. Worth every yen as a final meal.
Day 13 Departure Thursday, Aug 6
Morning
Final morning — konbini breakfast
7-Eleven, Lawson, or FamilyMart — Japan's convenience stores are genuinely excellent. Coffee from the machine, onigiri, tamagoyaki sandwich. Last walk if time allows.
10:00 am
Depart for airport — leave by 10am latest Allow 90+ min
KIX (Kansai International): Haruka Express from Tennoji or Namba (~70 min, JR Pass). ITM (Itami): Osaka Monorail connection or airport bus (~35 min). Factor in check-in, immigration, and security for an afternoon international flight. Do not cut it close.
